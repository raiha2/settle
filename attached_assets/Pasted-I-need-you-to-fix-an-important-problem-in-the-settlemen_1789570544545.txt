I need you to fix an important problem in the settlement logic of my bill-splitting app.

DO NOT just patch the UI. First understand and correct the underlying calculation logic.

## The problem

The app currently handles this example incorrectly:

* Total bill = Rs. 1,300
* Person 1 paid Rs. 1,000
* Person 2 paid Rs. 1,000
* Person 3 paid Rs. 0
* Total cash handed over = Rs. 2,000
* Change received = Rs. 700

The app correctly displays the Rs. 700 change, but the settlement summary is wrong.

It currently says Person 3, who paid nothing, should make payments to Person 1 and Person 2 and then Person 3 also receives part of the Rs. 700 change.

That is confusing and does not represent the real cash flow correctly.

## IMPORTANT CONCEPT

Separate these concepts:

### 1. Amount paid at the counter

This is the actual money each person contributed.

Person 1: Rs. 1,000
Person 2: Rs. 1,000
Person 3: Rs. 0

Total contributed = Rs. 2,000

### 2. Actual bill

Bill = Rs. 1,300

### 3. Change

Change = total contributed - bill

Rs. 2,000 - Rs. 1,300 = Rs. 700

The Rs. 700 is NOT an additional expense and should NOT be treated as a normal payment by the person who receives it.

It is returned cash from the original contributors' Rs. 2,000.

---

# HOW THE APP SHOULD THINK ABOUT IT

The final amount that actually needs to be paid for the expense is ONLY:

Rs. 1,300

The Rs. 700 change is returned money.

Therefore the app should determine how the Rs. 1,300 expense is ultimately shared among the 3 people.

Equal split:

Rs. 1,300 / 3 = Rs. 433.33 each

The app should then account for the fact that Person 1 and Person 2 initially put Rs. 1,000 each into the transaction and Rs. 700 of that money came back as change.

The app must assign the Rs. 700 change back to the people who contributed the money.

DO NOT simply give the entire Rs. 700 to Person 3.

---

# CHANGE OWNERSHIP

The change should be allocated to the people who paid for the original transaction.

In this example:

Person 1 contributed Rs. 1,000 / Rs. 2,000 = 50%

Person 2 contributed Rs. 1,000 / Rs. 2,000 = 50%

Therefore:

Person 1 receives Rs. 350 of the change.

Person 2 receives Rs. 350 of the change.

Person 3 receives Rs. 0 of the change.

After returning the change:

Person 1's effective contribution = Rs. 1,000 - Rs. 350 = Rs. 650

Person 2's effective contribution = Rs. 1,000 - Rs. 350 = Rs. 650

Person 3's effective contribution = Rs. 0

Total effective contribution:

Rs. 650 + Rs. 650 = Rs. 1,300

This exactly matches the bill.

---

# FINAL SETTLEMENT

Each person's share is:

Rs. 1,300 / 3 = Rs. 433.33 approximately.

After accounting for the change:

Person 1 effectively paid Rs. 650.

Person 2 effectively paid Rs. 650.

Person 3 effectively paid Rs. 0.

Therefore the remaining amounts are approximately:

Person 1 overpaid by Rs. 216.67

Person 2 overpaid by Rs. 216.67

Person 3 owes Rs. 433.33

The settlement should therefore be approximately:

Person 3 → Person 1: Rs. 216.67

Person 3 → Person 2: Rs. 216.66

The one-cent difference is simply due to rounding.

MOST IMPORTANTLY:

Person 3 should NOT:

1. Pay Person 1
2. Pay Person 2
3. Then receive part of the change

That creates a confusing circular-looking settlement.

The change should first be returned/allocated to the people who actually contributed the money.

Then calculate the final settlement using their effective contributions.

---

# GENERAL RULE

Please implement this as a general rule, not as a hardcoded fix for the Rs. 1,300 example.

If:

totalPaid > billAmount

then:

change = totalPaid - billAmount

The change belongs to the people who contributed to the payment.

Allocate the change proportionally according to each person's contribution:

changeForPerson =
(personPaid / totalPaid) * change

Then:

effectivePaid =
personPaid - changeForPerson

The effectivePaid amounts should add up exactly to the bill amount, subject to rounding.

Then calculate each person's share and settlement using effectivePaid.
